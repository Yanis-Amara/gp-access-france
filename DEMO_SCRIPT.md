# Demo video script

Target duration: approximately 3 minutes 15 seconds, including interactions.
Speak naturally and adapt the wording to your own voice.

## 00:00–00:25

**Show:** National overview; leave the department filter empty.

**Say:** Hello, I'm Yanis Amara, a Data Engineering student at EFREI. This project, for Dr. Mano Mathew's Data Analysis and Visualization course, explores potential access to general practitioners in France. My upcoming healthcare research internship motivated me to investigate what national averages can hide.

## 00:25–00:55

**Show:** Point to the APL definition and the national KPI cards.

**Say:** The source is DREES, and the scope is France excluding Mayotte. We focus on GPs aged sixty-five or younger. APL estimates annual accessible consultations per age-standardised inhabitant. It does not measure waiting times. In twenty twenty-four, national APL was three point two six, down one point zero nine percent from twenty twenty-three.

## 00:55–01:20

**Show:** Toggle Lowest/Highest APL, return to Lowest, then show the map.

**Say:** The ranking lets me compare the lowest and highest departmental values. The map adds geographical context, including overseas departments. Its colour scale stays fixed across views, so comparisons remain consistent. I can also restrict the analysis using the sidebar.

## 01:20–01:55

**Show:** Set Area to Guyane; show its KPIs and local distribution.

**Say:** Selecting Guyane changes the KPIs and all analytical views. Its APL is about one point four one. The local distribution shows what this average hides: twelve of twenty-two communes have zero APL for this GP age group, covering forty-seven thousand and seventy-seven residents, or sixteen point three percent. This does not mean those communes have no healthcare services.

## 01:55–02:25

**Show:** Return to France excluding Mayotte, clear Departments, and show the scatter plot. Hover over a point.

**Say:** Returning to the national selection, the scatter plot links current access with recent change. The lower-left area contains departments with below-national APL and a decline. Fifty departments meet both conditions, containing thirty-eight point one percent of the covered population. These are signals at department level, not individual assessments.

## 02:25–02:55

**Show:** Open the teleconsultation and policy-context notes.

**Say:** The notes explain the weighting, reference years and limits. Teleconsultations are included in APL activity, but this workbook cannot isolate their contribution or show the effect of platforms such as Doctolib. The policy initiatives discussed were introduced after the observations, so these data cannot evaluate their results.

## 02:55–03:15

**Show:** Show From evidence to local action.

**Say:** The takeaway is to start with national comparisons, inspect local differences and then collect information about appointments, staffing, transport and digital access. The dashboard supports local investigation rather than proving causes or identifying an official medical-desert category.
